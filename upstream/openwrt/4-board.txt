realtek: rtl930x: add support for MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM

The MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM is a managed switch with seven 10GBASE-T
ports with 802.3af/at/bt PoE and one 10G SFP+ port.

Specifications:
- SoC: Realtek RTL9303, 800 MHz MIPS 34Kc
- RAM: 128 MiB DDR3
- Flash: 16 MiB SPI NOR (Winbond W25Q128)
- Ethernet: 7x 10GBASE-T (three RTL8261BE, one RTL8264B), 1x 10G SFP+
- PoE: two Realtek RTL8239-VB PSE chips driven over I2C, up to 90 W per
  port, 260 W in total
- Fans: ADT7476 fan controller
- Buttons: reset
- LEDs: three per port on the switch's LED controller (orange and green
  link/activity, green PoE), system LED
- Power: internal AC power supply, 100-240 V
- UART: header J4 (GND, TXD, RXD, 3V3), 115200 8N1

The fan controller, the PoE chips and the SFP+ cage share one bit-banged
I2C clock line (GPIO 8), with SDA on GPIOs 9, 10 and 11. The PoE chips
need Realtek firmware loaded at power-up, which cannot be shipped: copy
it out of the vendor firmware and install it as
/lib/firmware/realtek/rtl8239-bt.bin. The PoE ports share the 260 W
supply as a power budget, with port priorities, and wait switched off
until netifd switches them on: board.json switches PoE on for lan1-lan7
by default (netifd's pse option, with netifd's board.json default for
it). The port LEDs are set up as the vendor SDK does (bi-colour scan
mode, three LEDs per port) instead of relying on the vendor U-Boot,
which sets them up as part of "rtk network on"; the PoE LEDs (LED 2 of
each port) have no hardware function and need software control, which
follows with openwrt/openwrt#25341. At boot, hwmon_fancontrol puts the
ADT7476 into its automatic mode with the vendor firmware's settings,
through adt747x_fancontrol(): 50 %, and full speed when the hottest of
its sensors reaches 66 C, with the critical limit left at the chip's
default of 100 C, as the vendor firmware leaves it.

MAC addresses:
- eth0, the lan interface and lan1: ethaddr in u-boot-env
- lan2-lan8: ethaddr with the locally administered bit set, plus 1 to 7
A missing ethaddr, or Realtek's default 00:e0:4c:00:00:xx, gives a
random address instead.

Installation from the vendor web UI:
Upload the factory image (*-squashfs-factory.bix) on the firmware
upgrade page. The vendor firmware only checks the uImage magic
(0x83800000) and the header and data CRCs, and writes the file to its
RUNTIME partition, which is OpenWrt's "firmware" partition.

Installation over the serial console:
1. Stop U-Boot with Esc and boot the initramfs image over TFTP from
   192.168.2.254 (the image renamed to initramfs.bin):
     rtk network on
     tftpboot 0x84f00000 initramfs.bin
     bootm 0x84f00000
2. Install the sysupgrade image with sysupgrade -n.

Back to the vendor firmware: write its image (a plain uImage) to the
"firmware" partition with mtd write. The vendor configuration
partitions are left untouched by OpenWrt.
