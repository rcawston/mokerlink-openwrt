net: phy: realtek: add RTL8261N and RTL8264B

The RTL8261N (also marked RTL8261BE) and the quad-port RTL8264B are
10GBASE-T PHYs that no mainline driver claims. The generic Clause 45
driver binds to them instead. It can't read the link partner's
1000BASE-T ability, which these PHYs keep in their vendor MMD, so a
1 Gb/s link comes up as 100 Mb/s. On an RTL9303 switch, whose MDIO
controller sets the MAC from the PHY in hardware, the MAC then runs at
1 Gb/s while phylib reports 100 Mb/s.

These two patches add an entry for each PHY with the RTL8261C/D
functions for features, autonegotiation and status, which read and
write 1000BASE-T at the same vendor MMD addresses as Realtek's own
driver for these PHYs [1]. Like that driver, they leave out the 10BASE-T
modes that the PMA reports.

The entries leave the PHY as the boot loader set it up, as the generic
driver does today: no firmware, reset, interrupts or suspend. Realtek's
driver patches the PHYs from register tables when they start, and
OpenWrt's [2] from firmware files, but linux-firmware has no firmware
for these PHYs, and a missing file shouldn't cost the link that works
now.

Each PHY ID is shared with a 5 Gbps part, the RTL8251L and the RTL8254B,
which OpenWrt's driver tells apart by the model register VEND1 0x103.
The entries leave those two parts to the generic driver: the RTL8261N
entry binds only to 0x8261, and the RTL8264B one, as in OpenWrt, to
anything but 0x8254.

Javen, does Realtek plan to support the RTL8261N and RTL8264B in this
driver, and to publish firmware for them in linux-firmware, as for the
RTL8261C/D? Is VEND1 0x103 the right way to tell them from the RTL8251L
and RTL8254B, and what does it read on the RTL8264B?

Merge path: both patches through net-next. They depend on nothing
outside it.

Testing: the results of the CI build of this version (W=1 and sparse,
for MIPS and x86_64) and of the hardware test on a MokerLink
POE-10G07110GSM (RTL9303, three RTL8261BE and one RTL8264B) go here
before this is posted.
