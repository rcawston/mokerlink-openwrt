net: ethernet: realtek: add an RTL930x switch driver

The Realtek RTL9301, RTL9302B, RTL9302C and RTL9303 are MIPS SoCs built
around an Ethernet switch with up to 28 ports. Mainline has the drivers
for the switch's MDIO and I2C controllers and its binding, but not for
the switch. This series adds the start of that driver: the DMA engine
through which the CPU reaches the switch, and the ports as standalone
network interfaces with phylink. Bridge offload, VLANs, the SerDes and
PCS, and the port LEDs follow in later series.

The hardware, in Linux terms:

        CPU (MIPS 34Kc)
           |
    +------+--------------------- switch block (one syscon) ---------+
    |      |                                                          |
    |  DMA engine ("NIC") --- port 28 --+                             |
    |   CPU tag in the                  |                             |
    |   descriptors           L2 switch +--- ports 0-27 --- SerDes ------ PHYs
    |                                   |                             |   |
    |  MDIO controller: polls the PHYs and sets the MACs from them -------+
    |  I2C controllers, reset                                         |
    +-----------------------------------------------------------------+

The DMA engine is part of the switch block and reaches nothing but the
switch's port 28. It is not an Ethernet controller, and the CPU tag (the
source port of a received frame, the destination ports of one to send)
exists only in its DMA descriptors, never in a frame. So this is a
switchdev driver that runs the DMA engine itself, as sparx5, lan966x and
ocelot's VSC7514 driver do, and not a DSA switch behind a conduit; Andrew
advised that for this chip when Chris asked in 2024 [1], and the binding
already describes it that way: the "nic" interrupt is on the switch node,
and there is no CPU port.

How the ports work:

- Each port with a PHY becomes an interface. Its MAC can't be given the
  speed or duplex of the PHY itself: the MDIO controller polls the PHYs
  in hardware and sets the MACs from that, once phylib attaches the PHY,
  which turns the polling of the port on in the MDIO driver. So the MAC
  is left unforced, and mac_link_up enables it only after checking that
  the MAC reports the link phylib does, as Andrew suggested on the MDIO
  driver; a MAC that disagrees stays off with an error, though phylink
  still reports the link up. Pause is forced to what phylink resolved.
- The PHY is attached when the port is created, not when the interface is
  opened, as DSA does, so that ethtool's PSE control works on a port that
  is down.
- The ports forward nothing between them: each one forwards only to the
  CPU port, nothing is learned, every port forwards in the one spanning
  tree instance, and VLANs neither filter nor change what reaches the
  CPU, apart from priority tags (VID 0), which are probably lost in both
  directions. Port mirroring, ACLs, trunks and routing, which the driver
  doesn't use, are left as they are and taken to be off, and the L2
  table is taken to hold no static entries.
- All ports share one TX ring of the DMA engine, so their queues stop
  together when it is full. OpenWrt only ever acknowledged the TX done
  interrupts, as it copies the frames it sends, so while frames are in
  the ring a timer also kicks the engine and the poll every tick, as
  stmmac's TX coalescing timer does.
- The RX ring holds 64 frames at any MTU: its counter must never let it
  overflow, even with frames of the longest length.

What it relies on: the SerDes of the ports are used as the boot loader set
them up, and the parts of the hardware polling setup that mainline's MDIO
driver doesn't do yet (the MAC type of each port, the polling of SerDes
ports) too. Ports without a PHY, such as SFP cages, and ports with
in-band status need the PCS that comes with the SerDes series, and are
skipped for now, as are interface modes other than SGMII, QSGMII,
USXGMII and 10G-QXGMII.

There is no public datasheet. The register layout and the order of the
setup follow OpenWrt's realtek target, by Birger Koblitz, Markus
Stockhausen, Jonas Jelonek, Gennaro Cimmino and other contributors.

Merge path: all patches through net-next. The series changes no binding
and depends on nothing outside net-next; the device tree of the
MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM goes to the MIPS tree separately.

Testing:
- Each patch builds with W=1 and sparse for MIPS; the series also builds
  with W=1 and sparse for x86_64 (COMPILE_TEST).
- On a MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM (RTL9303), booted from the vendor
  U-Boot after "rtk network on", with lockdep and DMA-API debugging. The
  test image adds PHY driver entries for the board's RTL8261N and
  RTL8264B, which net-next lacks and which are not part of this series:
  without them, phylib's generic Clause 45 driver binds and takes a
  1 Gb/s link for 100 Mb/s. With them:
  - all seven copper ports probe and attach their PHYs, and ethtool
    finds each PHY for PSE while its port is down;
  - links at 10 Gb/s on all seven ports, and at 2.5 Gb/s, 1 Gb/s and
    100 Mb/s on ports with each PHY; the carrier drops about a second
    after a cable pull, and comes back with the cable;
  - ping, VLAN-tagged frames both ways, and an 8972-byte ping with DF at
    MTU 9000; MTU 12262 is accepted;
  - frames leave at their own length, and the port counters agree with
    the host's;
  - iperf3 TCP at 112 Mb/s from the switch and 156 Mb/s to it, and UDP
    at 44 Mb/s from it (the CPU is an 800 MHz MIPS 34Kc);
  - the TX done interrupt fires, about once per frame while sending
    UDP, and the DMA engine raises no interrupt with every port down;
  - an ARP broadcast from a host on one port reaches no other port;
  - 100 down/up cycles of a port, and unbinding and binding the driver,
    without lockdep or DMA-API reports.
- Build-tested only on the RTL9301, RTL9302B and RTL9302C.
