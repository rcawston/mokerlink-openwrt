net: ethernet: realtek: rtl9300: add the ports

Every port of the ethernet-ports node with a PHY becomes a network
interface. Received frames go to the interface of the port in their CPU
tag, if it is up, and frames to send leave through the port of their
interface. Frames lost on the way to an interface that is up count as
its rx_dropped.

The link of a port is handled by phylink. The MAC can't be given the
speed and duplex of a PHY port itself: the MDIO controller polls the
PHYs in hardware and sets the MAC from that, once the PHY is attached,
which turns the polling of the port on in the MDIO driver. So the MAC
is left unforced, and mac_link_up only enables it, after checking that
the MAC reports the link phylib does, as Andrew Lunn suggested for this
hardware: a MAC that disagrees stays off with an error, rather than
passing traffic at the wrong speed; mac_link_up can't fail, so phylink
still reports the link as up. Pause is forced to what phylink
resolved, as OpenWrt does.

The PHY is attached when the port is created, not when the interface is
opened, as DSA does: a PHY's PSE control has to work while its interface
is down, and ethtool finds it through the attached PHY. As with DSA, the
PHY's sysfs links to the interface are missing, as the interface isn't
registered yet when the PHY is attached. Also as with DSA, the drivers
of the board's PHYs, and any firmware they load, have to be there when
the switch probes: built in, with the firmware in the initramfs, or as
modules loaded before the switch's. The switch waits for the PHY
devices, not for their drivers, and only until the deferred probe
timeout. The driver selects REALTEK_PHY, for the Realtek PHYs that
RTL930x boards are built with, so that a built-in switch gets a built-in
PHY driver rather than the generic one.

fw_devlink does not follow phy-handle, and the MDIO controller is a
child of the switch, so the driver defers its probe itself until the
PHYs of the ports, and the NVMEM cells of their MAC addresses, are
there, before it sets the hardware up; it only turns the MACs and the
DMA engine off first, in case the boot loader left them running. A PHY
that isn't there by the deferred probe timeout, or that fails to attach,
costs only its port, and so does a PHY on a disabled MDIO bus; a missing
NVMEM cell gives the port a random address. Ports without a PHY, such as
SFP cages, need the SerDes and PCS setup that comes in a later series,
and are skipped for now; so are interface modes other than SGMII,
QSGMII, USXGMII and 10G-QXGMII, and ports with in-band status. The
SerDes of the supported modes are used as the boot loader set them up.

All ports share the one TX ring, so when it is full the queues of all
open ports stop, as with lan966x and ocelot, and they wake together once
a quarter of the ring is free again. They also share the NAPI context,
which closing a port cycles, so that none of the port's frames, also
none that GRO holds, is delivered once the port is closed. Shutdown runs
the whole remove, so it now also unregisters the ports and detaches
their PHYs.
