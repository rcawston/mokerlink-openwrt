net: pse-pd: add Realtek RTL8239

The RTL8239 is an 8-channel IEEE 802.3af/at/bt PSE controller. Some
Realtek-based PoE switches, such as the MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM, drive
it directly over I2C, with no PSE microcontroller in front of it, so the
realtek-pse-mcu driver does not apply.

There is no datasheet. The register map follows TI's TPS2388x
(operating mode, power status, class, current and voltage, event
registers), but each 4-channel half answers on its own address with
8-bit access, and the chip runs Realtek firmware that the host loads at
power-up through a broadcast address. Without that firmware the chip
detects and powers nothing. The firmware is not redistributable; the
driver loads it with the firmware loader if it is installed, and looks
for it again periodically otherwise. Everything here follows the vendor
firmware of the MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM.

With a vpwr-supply on the PSE PIs, the driver supports the static
budget evaluation strategy: the chip detects and classifies a PD in
semi-automatic mode, and the core switches the port on once the power
of its class fits the budget. Without one, the chips run in auto mode
and power any valid PD on their own, as the vendor firmware runs them.

Tested on that switch with an 802.3bt access point (class 7) and an
802.3at camera (class 4) on both chips: detection, classification,
power, measurements, admin state and the PSE LED triggers.

One question for the budget: the two RTL8239s of that switch share one
54 V supply, so their PIs share one power domain, but the core only
sheds or retries PIs of the controller that reported the event. Should
the core walk all controllers of a power domain there, or should such a
board describe one supply per chip?

The driver uses devm_pse_poll_helper(), as the board does not wire the
chips' interrupt line, so this series depends on the PSE poll helper
series.
