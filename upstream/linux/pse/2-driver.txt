net: pse-pd: add Realtek RTL8239 driver

The RTL8239 is an 8-channel IEEE 802.3af/at/bt PSE controller. Some
Realtek-based PoE switches drive it directly over I2C instead of
through a PSE microcontroller. Its register map follows TI's TPS2388x,
with three differences that this driver handles:

- each 4-channel half answers on its own I2C address, the second at the
  first plus one, with 8-bit register access; the second half is an
  ancillary device of the first
- the chip runs Realtek firmware that the host sends at power-up, in
  one write to a broadcast address shared by all RTL8239s on the bus;
  the driver resets the chip, loads the image, waits for it to run, and
  reloads it if the chip stops running it. The image is looked for at
  probe and then periodically, so it can be installed later
- a port is switched on by putting its channels in auto mode and off by
  switching them off; detection, classification and 4-pair operation
  are left to the firmware

Without a power supply on the PSE PIs, all channels listed in the
device tree are put in auto mode at probe, before the controller is
registered, as the vendor firmware does, and the chips power any valid
PD on their own. With a vpwr-supply, the driver uses the static budget
evaluation strategy: the channels wait in semi-automatic mode, where
the chip detects and classifies a PD without powering it, the driver
reports a classified PD to the core, and the core switches the port on
(auto mode) once the power of its class fits the budget. A port that
loses power, or is not powered in time, goes back to waiting and is
reported as disconnected. Events come from the PSE poll helper. Power
status, class, voltage and power are reported as the vendor firmware
scales them.

There is no datasheet; the Realtek-specific registers (chip type,
firmware status and download) follow the vendor firmware of the
MokerLink POE-10G07110GSM, on which this was tested.
